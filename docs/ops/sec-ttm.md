# `landry.sec_ttm` and the trailing-12-month policy

<!-- Moved verbatim from CLAUDE.md on 2026-10-09 (token diet). CLAUDE.md names the files to read before touching each area. -->

- **`python -m landry.sec_ttm TICKER [--windows N] [--add-back YYYY-MM-DD:USD]` and `python -m landry.sec_ttm --holdings` (built 2026-10-05; both print and write nothing) build trailing-12-month Tier 1 inputs from SEC XBRL companyfacts and run the same rubric functions as `landry draft`.** The fiscal-year drafts can be up to a year behind the filings, which matters when a hard rule turns on them (AVGO and VRTX, Journal row 92). The windows are anniversary-aligned: the 12 months ending at the newest 10-Q quarter-end and the same 12 months one, two, three and four years earlier (five by default, because Part 2 says "five full fiscal years where available"), non-overlapping, TTM = FY + YTD - prior YTD; when the newest filing is a 10-K they are just the fiscal years. FCF is Part 10's, CFO - capex - capitalised software - stock-based compensation, and the tool refuses a window with no capex or SBC rather than assume zero. Windows older than the first complete run of filings are dropped (to a minimum of four) rather than failing, but a missing line item in the newest windows is an error. `--add-back` takes a documented one-time operating cash cost (Part 10 adjusts FCF "for acquisitions ... and other nonrecurring items", e.g. VRTX's $4.4B Alpine payment, which the FY24 10-K books as acquired IPR&D through operating cash flow): that is a judgment, so record it in the approval's `adaptation` field and say how much it moves the score; `landry_addbacks.json` is the registry the tool reads automatically (VRTX/Alpine is the only entry; `--no-registry` ignores it), so an approved add-back is not re-flagged every quarter. SEC's company-facts feed can trail EDGAR by a quarter (Visa's June 2026 10-Q was listed on EDGAR but missing from its facts on 10/5), so every run compares its newest window with EDGAR's newest 10-Q/10-K and warns when they differ. Scores still go through `ScoreStore.propose` / `approve` and the targeted `export_workbook` call like any other (the 10/5 re-score used source `quant_draft`, approved_by Alan on his instruction); a re-score also needs Monitor columns C-G stamped (`python -m landry monitor stamp`; the audit's `monitor_last_score` fails until it is), the Watch List Tracker row removed or updated, and the Journal entry. **The policy (Alan, 10/5/26, "go" on Open Items #31; Journal row 94):** fiscal-year scores stay the record; the latest 10-Q is a second opinion. At each quarterly review (a step in QTR-REVIEW-1/2 and FUND-REFRESH-1/2 in Process Checklist) and before acting on any Rule 1 / Rule 3 trip, Watch List or Exit Review deadline or Part 6 sell trigger, run `--holdings`: it compares every held, scored stock's three Tier 1 quant scores with five anniversary windows and, as a second opinion, the newest four. Re-score on trailing windows only where a hard-rule outcome or Decision band differs on BOTH counts (exit status 2; "knife-edge" means it differs on one only -- look, don't re-score), in either direction, with any Part 10 adjustment written down. It cannot do ASML and TSM (20-F filers: no 10-Q), REITs (PLD needs the AFFO adaptation), or filers whose XBRL lacks quarterly SBC or capex facts (GE, NVDA on 10/5) -- those it lists as BY HAND; check them from the filings. Don't apply the trailing basis to other names outside this process. Window count and anniversary alignment decide knife-edge results (AVGO's margin trend is -16% against a 10% tolerance on five windows, -10.3% on four, and 'stable' on an overlapping fiscal-year-plus-TTM series; ANET's first flag rested on a revenue-growth variability of 0.3501 against a 0.35 line), so choose the design before looking at the scores and show the variants. First run: VRT flagged (BUY -> STRONG BUY), ANET knife-edge -- Open Items #32. **Bug fixed 10/6/26 (found backfilling NFLX's Entry Checklist row): `ttm_windows` anchored its windows on the newest YTD period by end date only, and a 10-Q reports the quarter alone beside the six-month figure with the same end date -- when the SEC listing put the quarter last it computed FY + Q2 - prior Q2 and silently dropped Q1's change (NFLX TTM operating cash flow $9.47B against $11.97B in the filings; FCF yield 2.9% instead of 3.8%).** It now takes the longest period ending on the newest date; `tests/test_landry_sec_ttm.py` pins both orders. A sweep of the held names showed NFLX was the only wrong one, and the 10/5 comparison and the AVGO / VRTX re-scores were unaffected. **Second bug fixed 10/8/26 (found scoring RL): `_fiscal_year_ending_before` took the latest fiscal year ON FILE rather than the year ending just before the year to date, so a newest 10-K missing one line item silently borrowed an older year** (RL's FY2026 capex is absent from the SEC facts: trailing capex $82M against about $296M, FCF $1.12B against about $0.9B). A missing year now makes the newest window incomplete and the tool refuses ("supply the figure by hand"); the single-ticker command prints that as a message. Its reach was small: re-running the whole 322-name 10/8 screen changed 4 names (APD, LNT, LHX, RL), no pass/fail flipped, and the 10/5 holdings comparison reproduces unchanged (Journal rows 122-123). **The same NFLX data carries a one-time item the tool cannot see:** the $2.8B Warner Bros. termination fee (Q1 2026) sits in operating cash flow, so TTM FCF reads $10.66B against about $7.86B normalized -- pass a negative `--add-back 2026-03-31:-2800000000` (Part 10 nonrecurring) or normalize by hand until it ages out of the window (Q1 2027).
